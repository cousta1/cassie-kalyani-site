Hatha Yoga

Hatha Yoga is a complete system — working the body, the breath, and the mind as one. Every class includes postures (asanas), breathwork (pranayama), Yoga Nidra (yogic sleep), and meditation, so students leave refreshed, relaxed, anchored, and present for the demands of everyday life.

The classes are open to everyone who wants to improve their health. The sequences are designed with modern life in mind — addressing the stiffness and tension that build up from sitting for long hours, driving, and working at a desk. Each class works to improve posture, open areas of the body that tend to hold tightness, and restore a natural ease of movement. All sequences are crafted carefully and intelligently, so the body is always prepared for what comes next.

Breathwork is woven throughout. Conscious breathing strengthens the diaphragm — improving how efficiently oxygen moves through the body. Better oxygenation means clearer thinking, more stable energy, and a nervous system that can regulate itself rather than stay stuck in stress. Every class closes with Yoga Nidra — a practice of deep conscious rest that lowers cortisol, calms the nervous system, and over time, significantly improves the quality of sleep.

About

Cassie · Kalyāṇī

Kalyāṇī is the Sanskrit spiritual name given to Cassie by her teachers — meaning auspicious, beautiful, and blessed.

Kalyāṇī is a 200-hour Hatha Yoga teacher trained at the Integral Yoga Institute, part of one of the oldest authentic yoga lineages in the West, rooted in the teachings of Swami Satchidananda. She is also trained in Prenatal and Fertility Yoga, and teaches pranayama and meditation.

The practice

Why Hatha?

Hatha Yoga is the traditional foundation of yoga as we know it — a practice passed down from teacher to student across thousands of years. Long before it became a fitness trend, it was a complete spiritual discipline: a system of postures, breathwork, chanting, deep relaxation (Yoga Nidra), and meditation.

Hatha Yoga works with the body, the breath, and the mind — not as separate things, but as one integrated whole. Its ultimate aim is not flexibility or strength, but stillness. Because it is in stillness that something deeper becomes possible: the nervous system settles, the mind quiets, and you begin to access your inner self — a clearer, more grounded awareness of who you are and how you move through life.
